The agent makes it. A human checks it.

KASPER · starts 28:45 · 45 s

KASPER

Maker and checker. The agent makes the payment, a human checks it. And that's two traces.

[left]

Trace A, the maker. The claim came in, the agent asked to execute, the policy said a person decides. The agent records "input required", and its trace ends at the hand-off. Nothing moved.

[middle]

Then human time. Minutes, maybe hours. Nothing runs, nothing polls.

[right]

Trace B, the checker. A person approves in the reviewer UI. It asks the gateway to execute under its own identity and its own rule, human-approved.execute. The policy service checks the ledger, sees the payment waiting for a human, and permits.

"Nothing was overridden. A different caller was asked about."

[the dashed arrow]

The dashed line is a span link: B points back to A. A link, not a parent. Work separated by human time is two traces and a link. You saw it in the demo: the approval's trace has a link icon back to the agent's. And at the bottom, how long humans take: one histogram, p95 by autonomy level. Where the human stands is William's last word.

What the slide is for

It answers the auditor's third question, could a human stop it?, and shows how to trace it honestly. Maker/checker (four-eyes) is a control every bank and insurer already has. The slide shows that an agent fits into it without special casing: the human approval is just another governed caller with its own rule. Technically it introduces the fourth of the five OpenTelemetry words from the 1,000-mile slide: link.

The theory behind it

Plain words. A parent/child relation says "this happened inside that". A span link says "this is related to that". If you made the approval a child of the agent's work, the agent's trace would appear to run for three hours, and every duration chart would be wrong. Analogy: a parcel that sits in a depot overnight. The next morning's delivery is a new trip with the tracking number on the label, not one 14-hour drive.

Why two traces. A trace models one continuous piece of work. Human time breaks that: no process is running while the claim waits. A link keeps the causal connection without lying about duration or about what was executing. OpenTelemetry's spec describes links for exactly this: batch and asynchronous work that relates to other traces.

The precise chain (as built).

  • Trace A: execute_tool execute_payment is denied (settlement-agent.autonomous.execute_limit), the ledger marks the payment awaiting_approval, claims-agent parks the A2A task in input-required with northstar.a2a.parked_reason = awaiting_approval.
  • Trace B root: northstar.review.decision (SERVER, reviewer-ui), with one span link to the refusal span in trace A. Attributes: northstar.review.decision, …reviewer.id, …reviewer.spiffe_id, …wait.duration, …payment.id, northstar.claim.id.
  • The execute goes through agentgateway again; the decision span says permit, rule human-approved.execute, ledger.status = awaiting_approval.
  • northstar.a2a.resume resumes the task and links back to the parking span (northstar.a2a.link.reason = resume). The outcome is pushed to the customer's page; the browser's own northstar.claim.wait span is a third trace, linked to the submit.

Configuration and options

  • The approver's rule in platform/policy-decision-service/pds/rules.py: HUMAN_APPROVERS = {…/sa/reviewer-ui}; it may execute only a payment the ledger shows awaiting_approval. No limit on that rule, so the decision span has no limit.amount. The gateway CEL ceiling (≤ €50,000) still applies to reviewer-ui.
  • The histogram northstar.approval.wait.duration (unit s), created in apps/reviewer-ui/approval_metrics.py. Attributes: northstar.review.decision and northstar.policy.autonomy.level. The reviewer id is deliberately absent (one series per person is high cardinality). The value is the span's own wait.duration, read from the ledger's awaitingSince, so metric and span can't disagree. In Prometheus: northstar_approval_wait_duration_seconds_*, default buckets 0…10000 s.
histogram_quantile(0.95,
  sum by (le, northstar_policy_autonomy_level) (
    rate(northstar_approval_wait_duration_seconds_bucket[1h])))

That query answers "did widening the band from assisted to autonomous cut the human's load?" Compare the count of approvals per level, and the p95 wait.

Alternatives

  • Keep the trace open (a long-running parent span). Simple to view as one waterfall, but durations become meaningless and an unended span is lost if the process restarts.
  • Correlate only by claim id (no link). Works for search, but the causal edge is lost; a link lets Jaeger jump from B to A.
  • Workflow engines (Temporal, Argo Workflows, a BPM tool) own the wait and the resume. They give timeouts, escalation and durable state that Northstar lacks, and they emit their own traces; you would still link across the wait.

If someone asks

Is the reviewer authenticated?
No. northstar.review.reviewer.id is the name typed into the form, a claim on the span, not a verified credential. The SPIFFE ID on the span is reviewer-ui's own workload identity. What's governed is the execution: it runs as reviewer-ui, through the same gateway and policy service, under its own rule.
What if nobody approves?
The claim waits. There is no timeout, no expiry and no second approver in the demo. Reject is final and the customer is told the claim was declined.
Could the reviewer UI pay anything it likes?
Only a payment the ledger shows awaiting_approval, and never above the €50,000 gateway ceiling.
