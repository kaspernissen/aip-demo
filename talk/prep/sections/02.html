What the slide is for

An introduction, not a résumé reading. The bullet lists on the slide are there for people who want them; do not read them out. Keep it to about 20 seconds each. The useful part for the room is the split of the talk: William owns Decide (the guardrails), Kasper owns Trace and Prove (the demo, the telemetry, the evaluations). Saying who built the demo also tells the audience who to ask about it in Q&A.

Why the beginners line matters

The track is marked Advanced, but much of the room will be platform engineers who have never instrumented an agent. Promising that they will be able to read the traces lowers the barrier and sets an obligation the talk keeps: every trace slide later says how to read it ("indented means called by the line above, struck through means it never happened").

If someone asks

Where is the demo code?
The Northstar demo is a repository with the whole stack brought up on a local kind cluster by one script (scripts/setup-kind.sh). Decide before the talk whether and where it is shared, and give the same answer each time.
Verify before the talk William's lines are placeholders; William writes his own introduction. Check the title and bio lines on the slide are current for both speakers.
