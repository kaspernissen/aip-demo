Three things to remember

WILLIAM · starts 34:55 · 40 s

Manuscript

WILLIAM

If you keep three sentences, keep these.

One: guardrails live in the platform, not in the prompt. Identity, reach, limits, and a human where the rule says. And the agent cannot move any of them.

Two: instrument the decisions, not only the errors. A permit, a hold, a sign-off: those are spans with a rule, a limit and an identity, and their names are a published schema. Then the evidence is a byproduct of the checks.

Three: build it from upstream projects. CNCF and the Agentic AI Foundation already ship the pieces. The only part you write is your rules.

[pause, then the line from the cover]

"If you can't trace it, you can't govern it."

What the slide is for

The takeaway slide; the one people photograph. Each point is actionable on Monday, and each has a concrete artefact from the demo behind it. The closing line repeats the cover's subtitle so the talk ends where it began.

Each point, with what backs it

1 · Guardrails live in the platform

Backed by the rings: SPIFFE/SPIRE (identity), Linkerd + NetworkPolicy (reach), agentgateway + policy service + OpenFeature (capability), Kyverno (keystone). The demo's Tricky claim showed the point directly: an instruction in the customer's text did not change what the gateway allowed. Monday action: list where your agent's limits live today. Anything that exists only in a system prompt is a suggestion.

2 · Instrument decisions, not only errors

Most services emit a span when something fails. Governance needs a span when something is decided, including "permit". The slide's example, northstar.policy.decision deny · 6000, is the record that answers "who allowed it, within what limits". The names are defined in a Weaver registry and checked in CI and live. A refusal is not an ERROR; it's a decision with a rule. Monday action: find the code path where your platform says yes or no to an agent, and make it emit a span with rule, limit, caller and outcome. Turn off sampling for it.

3 · Build it from upstream projects

Everything on the toolbox slide except the policy service's rules is an open project: SPIFFE/SPIRE, Linkerd, Kyverno, OpenFeature, OpenTelemetry (Collector, Weaver, semconv), Jaeger, Prometheus (CNCF); agentgateway, MCP and A2A (Agentic AI Foundation); AgentEvals (open source). The repo's governing rule: where a convention gap exists, report it upstream rather than inventing a private workaround. Monday action: before writing a governance component, check whether one of these already does it; write only the rules.

Alternatives and honest caveats

  • Managed agent platforms bundle identity, gateways, guardrails and tracing. Faster to start; the boundary then lives in a vendor's control plane and its telemetry may not follow open conventions.
  • What this does not do (keep ready for Q&A): no data ring (which records an agent may read), no authenticated reviewer, no timeout or second approver, no tamper-evident storage, and nothing on the platform side stops a believable lie under the limit.
