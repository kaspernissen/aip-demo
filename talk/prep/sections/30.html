What the slide is for

This is the trace the audience saw for a few seconds in Jaeger during the demo, redrawn so the room can read it. It answers the auditor's first question, what did the agent do?, in one picture, and it introduces the talk's least intuitive idea: a missing span can be proof. The audience should leave knowing how to read a trace waterfall (indentation = parent/child) and that a refusal shows up as a decision, not an error.

It follows "One trace id, every hop" (how the id got across) and sets up "The agent makes it. A human checks it." (what happens after the deny, in a second trace).

The theory behind it

Plain words. A trace is a family tree of work. Every step (a span) records who did it, when, how long it took and some facts about it. A child span sits under its parent, indented. If you know a step must produce a child span whenever it runs, then the lack of that child says the step didn't run. Think of a door with a turnstile counter: if the counter didn't move, nobody walked through.

Why "deny" is not ERROR. A refusal is the platform working correctly. If the decision span were marked ERROR, on-call would get paged for correct behaviour, and error-rate dashboards would rise every time the guardrail did its job. So northstar.policy.decision leaves span status Unset on a deny. The agent's own execute_tool execute_payment span is ERROR, because from the agent's point of view the tool call failed.

"The policy said no" vs "the policy service was down". The gateway's Guardrail CLIENT span carries grpc.status = 0: the call to the decision service worked, and the deny is in the payload. An unreachable policy service under failClosed shows a different gRPC status. Same refusal for the agent, two very different stories for the operator.

HTTP 200 means no. The gateway's tools/call SERVER span shows http.response.status_code = 200 even on a deny. MCP returns the refusal as a tool result marked isError inside a successful HTTP response. That span does carry error=true, otel.status_code=ERROR and a [policy-denial rule=… limit=… requested=…] tag in its status description. Anyone alerting on HTTP status alone will miss every refusal.

Measured detail. Trace 703c00f1880d8973b696a2c257ccf1be (2026-10-27, CLM-INTAKE-0E6260, autonomous, €8,500 held at €6,000): 304 spans, 148 of them from linkerd-proxy. The slide hides proxy spans and A2A plumbing. Per settlement there are two or three decision spans, one per tool call: a refused settlement has three (propose permit, execute deny, refuse permit). The slide's last line, refuse_payment permit, is the third.

One gap to know. The decision span carries no northstar.claim.id: agentgateway forwards only traceparent to the guardrail hook, never baggage. The decision is joined to the claim by trace id. That's why the Jaeger fallback searches by service and operation, not by the claim tag on the decision span.

Configuration and options

  • No sampling, anywhere. The Python services use the SDK default (parent-based, always on), and tests/unit/test_no_sampling.py asserts nobody configured otherwise. agentgateway's frontendPolicies.tracing has randomSampling: true; Linkerd's proxy has no sampler of its own and follows the incoming traceparent (deploy/platform/linkerd/values.yaml).
  • The collector (deploy/observability/collector-config.yaml) has only resource_detection and batch processors on traces. No filter, no tail sampler. Traces fan out to Jaeger, Weaver live-check and AgentEvals.
  • The decision span's attributes are declared once in pds/telemetry.py (ATTRIBUTES) and defined in telemetry/registry/policy.yaml. A unit test asserts the emitted set equals the declared set.
  • The shape is tested. tests/e2e/test_policy_denial.py pins the gateway SERVER span's children by exact equality: Guardrail (CLIENT) and northstar.policy.decision (SERVER) as siblings, and no CLIENT span to payments-mcp.

Alternatives

  • Head sampling (e.g. 10%). Cheap, standard, and it breaks this slide: a missing execute span could mean "never happened" or "not sampled". For decision spans that is hard to defend when the right to an explanation applies to each decision.
  • Tail sampling in the collector (keep every trace that contains a northstar.policy.decision or an error). Workable in production: keeps all governed traces, drops routine noise. Cost: the collector must buffer whole traces, and the rule set becomes something you have to audit too.
  • A hand-written audit log. Easy to read, but it records what someone remembered to log, and it can't show an absence. It also drifts from what the code actually did.
  • Marking deny as ERROR. Simpler to query, but it pollutes error SLOs and hides real faults among correct refusals.

If someone asks

Isn't 100% tracing too expensive in production?
Keep every decision span; that's the evidence. Sample the rest if you must, tier retention, and move message content to cheaper storage with separate access control. In the demo, 100% is asserted by a test.
Why is the agent's execute span red but the decision isn't?
The agent's tool call failed from its point of view. The decision service did its job. ERROR is reserved for faults.
